## Visual thesis
- (sebastian-cox) Sebastian Cox presents furniture and material practice as an ecological argument — the site reads as a quiet, light-filled workshop catalogue where natural textures, long-form essay copy, and unhurried photography insist that each piece is inseparable from the coppiced woodland it came from.
- (norm-architects) Norm Architects positions Nordic restraint as ethical sensibility — the site is a precisely proportioned, near-silent portfolio where the ratio of white space to content is itself a design argument, and every project appears as a self-sufficient object of calm.
- (shinola) Shinola frames Detroit-made goods as a moral proposition — the site functions as a slow-turning catalogue where rich product photography and plainspoken copy insist that where and how things are made is as important as what they look like.
## Typography
- (sebastian-cox) Body text in a humanist serif — a face close to Freight Text or a comparable book-weight transitional — at 17–19 px with generous line-height of 1.75–1.85, creating a reading cadence closer to a craft essay than a product listing. Headlines use a lighter-weight version of the same or a companion serif at 32–52 px with 1.1–1.2 leading. Navigation and labels appear in a small, understated sans-serif (geometric or humanist) at 12–14 px, weight regular, minimal tracking — it functions as a quiet system font backdrop to the more characterful serif content. No bold-weight display headlines; hierarchy is achieved through size alone, keeping tone measured and academic rather than promotional.
- (norm-architects) Entirely in a single geometric sans-serif family — GT America or a close equivalent (possibly a custom Norm variant) — deployed in regular and light weights only. Body text at 15–16 px, line-height 1.65–1.70, tracking 0. Project titles in the same face at 24–36 px, weight light, with normal (not tight) leading — no compression of type. Navigation and metadata labels at 12–13 px, regular weight, tracking +0.05–0.08 em, appearing as hairline-weight annotations. The typographic palette is a single family, two weights: the entire visual personality comes from size and spatial placement, not from decorative type. No serifs, no display fonts, no italic emphasis in the UI.
- (shinola) Primary display in a slab-serif or sturdy transitional (historically a custom Canela-influenced serif or similar workhorse face) at 36–60 px for hero headlines, lending the page a letterpress-poster quality. Body copy in a clean, slightly wide sans-serif (similar to GT America or a humanist grotesque) at 15–17 px with line-height 1.6–1.7. Navigation and product labels use a tracked all-caps sans at 11–12 px with +0.12–0.15 em tracking — an American workwear sensibility translated into digital. Price and product-name hierarchy relies on size contrast alone: name at 18–22 px regular weight, price at 14 px lighter weight below it, no bold emphasis needed.
## Color
- (sebastian-cox) Near-white linen ground (#F8F5F0 or #F4F1EB), very dark warm charcoal text (#2A2420 or #1E1A18). Single accent: a muted sage-green or woodland moss (#6B7B5E or #7D8B6A) used on hover states, active navigation, and the occasional rule — directly referencing the greenwood material. Photography supplies all warmth: oak grain, ash white, charcoal-dyed linen, forest bark. Palette is intentionally narrow at 3–4 values; the material world of the photography does all the colour work. No greys from a digital palette — only warm or cool-neutral tones derived from paper and wood.
- (norm-architects) Background: pure or near-pure white (#FFFFFF or #FAFAF9). Text: a soft charcoal, not full black (#2B2B2B or #333130), reducing the harshness of contrast and keeping the page tonally warm. Accent: none persistent — the only colour in the UI is the occasional muted warm grey (#D8D4CE) used for dividing rules and hover states. All colour enters through project photography, which is consistently warm-light Scandinavian interiors: natural oak, linen, sand, pale stone. The UI itself is intentionally colourless so that each project's material world reads without competition.
- (shinola) Foundation is a rich, slightly warm off-white (#F7F4EF or #F2EFE9) against near-black ink (#1C1C1A). Accent is a deep navy or dark slate (#1B2A3B), used for CTA buttons and select section backgrounds — never a bright primary. Secondary accent: a muted gold-tan (#B8945A) used sparingly in typographic rules and iconography, echoing strap leather and watch dials. Product photography dominates with dark studio tones, warm leather browns, brushed steel — the page's colour mood is built by the catalogue imagery, not the UI. Maximum 4–5 palette values; no saturated or digital hues.
## Layout tension / signature element
- (sebastian-cox) Generous single-column editorial sections for essay text alternate with asymmetric image-text splits — typically a wide photograph occupying 55–65% of the viewport width alongside a slimmer column of prose. The signature element is the **material footnote**: small-type annotations beneath product or process photographs that name the specific species of wood, its provenance, and the method of preparation — a taxonomic rigour that signals genuine craft knowledge rather than lifestyle branding. Max content width ~1200 px; side margins relaxed at ~8–10%. Product listings use a spare 2–3 column grid with wide gutters (40–56 px), each image occupying full card width with no overlay text.
- (norm-architects) A tight, invisible 12-column grid at ~1400 px max-width with generous but consistent side margins (~6–8%). The signature element is **radical asymmetric image sizing**: project listing pages position images at non-standard widths — one image spanning 7 of 12 columns while an adjacent one spans 4, creating an off-balance pairing that prevents the grid from feeling mechanical. This asymmetry is structural, not gestural. Within project pages, a single large photograph occupies the full viewport width before any text appears — the image as proposition, not illustration. Text is then set in a narrow central column (~40–50% of page width), preserving margins as breathing space.
- (shinola) Full-bleed editorial sections alternate with tighter product-grid modules: wide two-column editorial rows (copy left, full-bleed image right, or inverted) broken by 3–4 column product grids with generous padding (~24–32 px gaps). The signature element is the **origin-story banner** — a dark-background typographic stripe carrying a short declarative statement about Detroit manufacturing that appears between catalogue sections, anchoring the commerce in narrative. Max-width ~1440 px with 5–7% side margins on large desktop; product cards are borderless with no shadow, separated only by whitespace.
## Motion
- (sebastian-cox) Extremely restrained. Scroll-triggered image reveals fade in over 600–700 ms with a very gentle upward drift (~15 px translate) — unhurried and organic. No hover animation on product images beyond a subtle 1.0 → 1.02 scale at 300 ms ease-out. Navigation transitions at 200 ms opacity. Page transitions (if any) are simple cross-fades at 400 ms. The restraint mirrors workshop values: deliberate action, no superfluous movement. Zero parallax, zero scroll-jacking, zero auto-play.
- (norm-architects) Near-imperceptible. Page transitions are a clean 300 ms cross-fade. Scroll-triggered project images fade in at 400–500 ms with a very slight upward drift (10–15 px), ease-out cubic. Hover on project images: a gentle 1.0 → 1.03 scale at 300 ms ease — barely visible but present. No parallax. No scroll-jacking. No entrance animations on text — copy appears already in place. The site moves at the pace of architecture: unhurried, deliberate, with nothing needing to announce itself.
- (shinola) Conservative and purposeful. Hero images fade in at 400–500 ms ease-in-out on page load. Product image hover triggers a second-image cross-fade (alternate product angle) at 250–300 ms ease — a functional preview, not decoration. Scroll-triggered section reveals use a subtle upward translate (~20 px) + fade over 500 ms. No parallax, no scroll-jacking. CTA button hover fills with a 200 ms background-color ease. Overall pace is unhurried — consistent with a brand that sells watches and asks you to slow down.
## Signature moves
- (sebastian-cox) - **Essay-length process writing alongside products**: collection pages include 200–400-word essays on coppicing, material sourcing, or making process — set in the body serif with no subheadings — so buying a piece means reading about the woodland it came from. - **Species and provenance captions**: every timber photograph is labelled with the exact wood species and geographic origin in small secondary-type, treating botanical specificity as a design element rather than a footnote. - **Workshop photography as primary image grammar**: images are shot in natural light inside the actual workshop — sawdust, shavings, hand tools in frame — rather than studio white-seamless product shots, communicating authenticity over aspiration. - **Minimal navigation as editorial stance**: the main nav carries 5–6 items maximum, no dropdowns, communicating that the practice is deliberately small in scope and curated rather than comprehensive.
- (norm-architects) - **Asymmetric-column image pairing**: project thumbnails on listing pages are deliberately mismatched in column span (e.g., 7-col + 4-col, separated by 1-col gap), creating compositional tension that mirrors the studied asymmetry of Norm's interior photography. - **Full-bleed opening image with no UI chrome**: each project page opens with a single photograph occupying 100 vw × 85–95 vh before any header or text is visible, establishing the project's atmosphere before the concept statement. - **Metadata as spatial typography**: project details (year, type, location, photography credit) are rendered in the smallest type size as a left-anchored column annotation, creating a structured but unobtrusive information layer. - **Photography system consistency**: all project images use the same warm, high-key natural-light treatment — never a dark or dramatic contrast shot — so the portfolio reads as a unified visual language rather than a collection of disparate commissions.
- (shinola) - **Made-in-Detroit provenance callout**: a recurring text module (sometimes a full-width dark band) that states manufacturing location and team, treating factory origin as a headline feature — not tucked into an "About" page. - **Dual-image product hover**: hovering a product card swaps to a second studio image showing the reverse, strap detail, or movement — communicating craft transparency at thumbnail scale. - **Letterpress-inspired typographic hierarchy**: slab-serif display headlines paired with tracked-caps sans labels echo early-20th-century American job-printing, giving digital pages a tactile, ink-on-paper quality. - **Editorial story inserts**: between product rows, short editorial sections (~80–120 words of copy alongside a full-bleed shop-floor or artisan photograph) treat the catalogue as a magazine, not a store.
## Anti-patterns
- (sebastian-cox) Rejects lifestyle staging with interior-design room sets, auto-playing video tours, discount badges or seasonal sale banners, star-rating displays, "Add to Cart" with a loud CTA button colour, dark mode, stock photography of forests (all imagery is directly from their own woodland and workshop), feature-comparison tables, social-proof counters, and any visual language — gradients, geometric pattern fills, neon accents — that would signal the broader homeware mass-market. Rejects sans-serif body text; the editorial register requires a reading serif.
- (norm-architects) Rejects all decorative elements: no iconography beyond the most minimal navigation marker, no gradient fills, no texture overlays, no coloured backgrounds, no hero text animations, no slide-in transitions, no counters or statistics treated as graphic elements. Does not use dark mode. Rejects multi-column body text, pull quotes in large type, or any layout device that fragments the reading experience. Rejects lifestyle photography with people posed in spaces — rooms appear uninhabited, emphasising material and light over occupancy. No dropdown navigations, no mega-menus.
- (shinola) Rejects flash-sale banners, countdown timers, bright orange/green CTA buttons, auto-playing lifestyle videos, star-rating aggregates displayed prominently on listing pages, parallax hero effects, neon accent colours, condensed sans-serif display type, heavy drop shadows on product cards, aggressive upsell modals, and any visual language that signals fast-fashion disposability. Does not use a dark mode. Rejects layout conventions borrowed from tech SaaS products.
